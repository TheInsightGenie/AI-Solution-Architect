Yields and the AI Buildout

The Fed has cut 125bp since September 2024. Over the same window the 10-year Treasury yield rose ~95bp, to its highest level since November 2023. Big tech is earning more than ever from AI — and is now issuing enough debt to fund it that it has become part of what sets the long rate it gets discounted at.

Exhibit 1 · the divergence

Policy eased at the front end. The long end went the other way.

Both series are annualised percent, plotted on one scale, at month-end. Note the sign change: through late 2024 policy sat above the 10-year — an inverted curve. It crossed over in December 2024, and the gap has widened in the other direction for most of 2026. That gap is term premium: what investors charge for duration risk, on top of the expected path of policy.

Discount rate rises

AI earnings arrive far out in time, so they lose the most present value when rates rise.

30Y at 5.31%, a 19-year high
The loop closes: a higher discount rate raises the cost of the next round of capex financing, which pushes companies to issue more — or to spend less. Which of those two they choose is the question the 2026 equity market is actually pricing.

Reading the dashboard

What would change the picture

Watch next

  • FOMC, 15–16 Sep 2026. Three members dissented in July preferring a hike. A hawkish turn moves the front end up to meet the long end.
  • Q3 capex guidance. Microsoft's FY27 guide of $255–260B is the single largest number in the complex.
  • Issuance calendar. Roughly $334B of the expected 2026 AI debt had not yet priced as of end-May.

What breaks the loop

  • Capex discipline. One major hyperscaler guiding spending down would relieve both the FCF drain and the issuance pipeline.
  • Inflation cooling. It is elevated inflation, not growth, keeping the Fed on hold; a downside surprise lets the front end fall.
  • AI revenue catching capex. The gap between the two is the whole bear case.

Read this as a relationship map, not a live terminal. Correlation across 24 monthly observations does not establish causation, and the capex-to-yields channel described in Exhibit 3 is one mechanism among several — fiscal deficits and inflation expectations also carry term premium. The chart shows the two series moving apart; the mechanism is the argued explanation for it.

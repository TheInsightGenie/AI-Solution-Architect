What this replaces

Reading 47 contracts for change-of-control provisions is roughly three days of associate time. The abstraction ran in eleven minutes overnight.

It is a first pass, not an opinion. The value is that the lawyer starts from a cited table instead of a pile of PDFs — and can see exactly which entries need a human eye.

How this is built

The legal product on the left, the platform primitive on the right.

The architecture is borrowed from Cloudflare OS — an agent workspace where every user runs their own sandboxed copy of an app, and agents reach the outside world only through narrow, logged, approval-gated connectors.

That model happens to fit a law firm almost exactly. Capability-based access is an ethical wall. Human-in-the-loop approval is partner sign-off. An append-only action log is the audit trail a regulator or an insurer will ask for. The mapping below is the whole idea.

Why the sandbox matters here

A single shared AI assistant with firm-wide access is one prompt away from crossing an ethical wall. Giving every lawyer their own sandboxed instance with nothing but the resources they were explicitly handed makes the bad outcome structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged — which is the only version a risk partner will sign off.

Two senses of “abstraction”

Abstraction (the screen) is the legal one — reducing a contract to its key terms with a citation for each. Builder is the technical one — hiding the agent machinery behind blocks a lawyer can rearrange without an engineer. The product needs both: the first is the output they want, the second is the control they need over how it is produced.
